# A Computational Pipeline for Assigning Hemilineages to Neurons in MCFO Light-Microscopy Images of the Drosophila VNC

## TL;DR
- The most reliable route today is **not** to build a de-novo hemilineage classifier from scratch, but to **register MCFO VNC stacks to the JRC2018_VNC_Unisex template, segment single neurons, and match them (by NBLAST + color-depth MIP / PatchPerPixMatch) against the MANC male-VNC connectome — which carries curated `hemilineage`, `somaNeuromere`, and `entryNerve` annotations — then transfer the matched neuron's hemilineage label**; the primary-neurite tract/bundle entry point is the single strongest morphological feature for disambiguation.
- All major building blocks already exist as open tools (navis/natverse, flybrains, neuprint-python, NeuronBridge, PatchPerPixMatch, CMTK/ANTs, MANC neuprint dataset, JRC2018 templates), and there are exactly **34 major recognized adult VNC hemilineages** (Harris et al. 2015, eLife 4:e04493) with a mature nomenclature (Truman/Doe → Harris et al. 2015 → Court et al. 2020 → MANC), so the work is largely integration plus building a labeled reference library rather than fundamental invention.
- A published, drop-in "hemilineage classifier from LM images" does not exist as of September 2026; the novel component you must develop is the feature extractor + classifier that maps a segmented LM neuron to a hemilineage using tract entry, soma neuromere/position, and morphology similarity, validated against MANC/FANC ground truth.

## Key Findings
- **Ground truth is solid and connectome-anchored.** The MANC (Male Adult Nerve Cord) connectome has systematic, expert-curated hemilineage annotations exposed via neuPrint fields, and it is bridgeable into JRC2018_VNC light-level space, making it usable as a reference library. FANC provides an independent second reference.
- **Nomenclature is settled enough to build labels.** Harris et al. 2015 report that "the ventral nervous system of the adult fly is organized into 34 hemilineages," produced by ~24–25 adult-specific neuroblasts per thoracic hemineuromere. Hemilineages are named by lineage number + A/B (Notch-ON = "A" / Notch-OFF = "B"), e.g. 0A, 1B, 6A, 8B, 12A, 19A/19B, 23B. Court et al. 2020 fixed neuropil/tract nomenclature.
- **Tract/bundle entry is the killer feature.** Neurons of one hemilineage share a common primary neurite bundle and cell-body cluster; this is used by expert annotators and should be your highest-weight feature.
- **Matching infrastructure is mature.** NeuronBridge (color-depth MIP search + PatchPerPixMatch) and NBLAST (Costa et al. 2016, Neuron 91(2):293–311; with LM↔EM "synapse-blind" usage) already do LM↔EM neuron matching at scale.
- **The missing piece** is an end-to-end, automated hemilineage assignment tool with calibrated confidence; you will build this on top of the above.

## Details

### 1. Biological background and ground-truth resources

**Hemilineage concept.** The adult VNC is generated by a stereotyped set of adult-specific neuroblasts (NBs) — about **24–25 per thoracic hemineuromere** (Truman et al.; Harris et al. 2015). Most NBs divide in a Type I mode producing ganglion mother cells (GMCs); each GMC divides once, and Notch signaling makes the two daughters distinct, generating two **hemilineages** per lineage: **Notch-ON ("A")** and **Notch-OFF ("B")**. One hemilineage often undergoes programmed cell death, so not every lineage contributes two surviving adult hemilineages. **Exactly 34 major adult hemilineages** are recognized in the VNC (Harris et al. 2015, eLife 4:e04493). Each hemilineage tends to be developmentally and functionally coherent: a single fast neurotransmitter identity (ACh, GABA, or glutamate), a shared transcription-factor code, a shared primary neurite tract, and clustered cell bodies.

**Nomenclature and mapping.** The current standard names hemilineages by **lineage number + A/B** (e.g., 0A, 1B, 6A, 8B, 12A, 19A/19B, 23B). This descends from the Truman/Doe adult-specific lineage numbering, was extended to hemilineages by **Harris et al. 2015 (eLife 4:e04493), "Neuron hemilineages provide the functional ground plan for the Drosophila ventral nervous system,"** and was harmonized with neuropil/tract nomenclature by **Court et al. 2020 (Neuron), "A systematic nomenclature for the Drosophila ventral nerve cord."** There are older lineage-name synonyms (embryonic NB names like NB3-1, NB7-1, and clonal/technical names) that map onto the numbered lineages; Lacin & Truman 2016 and the MANC annotation papers provide the crosswalk.

**Key characterizing papers.**
- Truman et al. — developmental architecture of adult-specific lineages in the VNC.
- Shepherd et al. — developmental organization of central neurons in the adult VNC (Neural Development).
- **Harris et al. 2015, eLife 4:e04493** — 34 hemilineages, functional ground plan (per-hemilineage optogenetic/thermogenetic activation phenotypes).
- **Lacin & Truman 2016, eLife** — mapping embryonic to adult hemilineages via transcription-factor markers.
- **Lacin et al. 2019, eLife, "Neurotransmitter identity is acquired in a lineage-restricted manner"** — each hemilineage uses one fast neurotransmitter; TF codes.
- **Mark et al. 2021, eLife** — developmental framework linking neurogenesis and circuit formation.
- Newer (2023–2026): the MANC annotation papers (Marin et al. 2024; Takemura et al. 2024) and associated developmental/connectomic work extend and validate hemilineage assignments at connectome scale.

**Connectome ground truth.**
- **MANC** (Janelia FlyEM). The dense connectome is **Takemura et al. 2024, eLife 13:RP97769, "A connectome of the male Drosophila ventral nerve cord,"** and systematic annotation is **Marin et al. 2024, eLife 13:RP97766, "Systematic annotation of a complete adult male Drosophila nerve cord connectome reveals principles of functional organisation."** MANC provides curated per-neuron `hemilineage`, `somaNeuromere`, `entryNerve`, `class`, and systematic type. Hemilineage assignment in MANC combined soma cluster location, primary neurite tract, and comparison to known lineage clones/light-level references. Accessible via neuPrint (`manc` dataset) and `neuprint-python`.
- **FANC** (Female Adult Nerve Cord) — Phelps et al. 2021 (Cell) reconstruction; Azevedo et al. 2024 (Nature) connectome; FANC also carries hemilineage annotations and serves as an independent reference.

**Driver lines.** Hemilineage-specific and lineage-restricted split-GAL4 / GAL4 lines exist (Harris et al. 2015 and follow-ups; Janelia/Truman collections), and the FlyLight resource contains MCFO imagery for many. These are valuable both as additional labeled training data and for experimental validation.

### 2. Image data and preprocessing

**MCFO.** MultiColor FlpOut (**Nern, Pfeiffer & Rubin 2015, PNAS 112(22):E2967–E2976, "Optimized tools for multicolor stochastic labeling reveal diverse stereotyped cell arrangements in the fly visual system"**) uses stochastic FLP-mediated recombination to express spectrally/epitope-distinct membrane reporters in random subsets of a GAL4 pattern, revealing single-neuron morphology. Standard readout uses **epitope tags (HA, V5, FLAG**, and additional tags in expanded versions) detected by immunostaining, plus a **neuropil counterstain (nc82 / anti-Bruchpilot)** used as the registration reference channel. Typical data are multi-channel confocal stacks; single neurons are separated by their distinct tag/color combinations, though labeling is often partial and multiple neurons can share a channel.

**Public MCFO VNC datasets.**
- **Janelia FlyLight** Split-GAL4 and Gen1 MCFO collections (Meissner et al. 2023, eLife image resource) — includes VNC imagery and annotations; downloadable via Janelia Open Data / S3 (flylight-color-depth and related buckets).
- **NeuronBridge** hosts color-depth MIPs for these collections.
- **Virtual Fly Brain (VFB)** provides registered imagery, ontology, and cross-links.
Metadata (driver, sex, imaging template, channels) accompany downloads.

### 3. Registration / template alignment

**Templates.** Use **JRC2018_VNC_UNISEX** (and _FEMALE/_MALE) from **Bogovic et al. 2020, PLOS ONE 15(12):e0236495, "An unbiased template of the Drosophila brain and ventral nerve cord."** Symmetrized versions exist and are convenient for left-right feature normalization.

**Tools.** CMTK and ANTs are the workhorse intensity-based registration engines (Janelia's pipeline uses CMTK); elastix and BigWarp (landmark/manual) are alternatives. In R: `nat`, `nat.templatebrains`, `nat.jrcbrains`, `natverse`. In Python: `navis`, `flybrains` (navis-flybrains), `fafbseg`. Bridging registrations between JRC2018_VNC and EM spaces (MANC, FANC) are distributed with flybrains / the malevnc resources and applied via **`navis.xform_brain`** (Python) or `xform_brain` (R). This lets you pull MANC EM skeletons into JRC2018_VNC_Unisex to build an LM-comparable reference library.

**Practical guidance.** Register each MCFO stack to JRC2018_VNC using the **nc82 channel** as the moving-to-fixed reference; verify with landmark overlays; then apply the same transform to the signal channels so segmented neurons live in template space with comparable coordinates for soma position and tract entry.

### 4. Segmentation / single-neuron extraction

Approaches, roughly increasing in automation:
- **Color-depth MIP** (Otsu; Meissner et al.) — encodes depth as hue on a MIP; the basis of color-depth search.
- **Manual/semi-automatic tracing** — Simple Neurite Tracer / **SNT** in Fiji; Vaa3D + APP2 / neuTube for automated tracing/skeletonization.
- **ML segmentation** — Ilastik (pixel classification), U-Net / 3D U-Net / nnU-Net for semantic segmentation of neurite signal.
- **Purpose-built instance segmentation** — **PatchPerPix** (Mais, Hirsch, Kainmueller) for instance segmentation of neurons, and **PatchPerPixMatch (PPPM)** for automated 3D matching of LM neuronal morphologies to EM, integrated into NeuronBridge. This is the most directly relevant modern tool for pulling individual neurons out of MCFO stacks and matching them.

Output should be per-neuron masks → skeletons (SWC) in template space.

### 5. Morphology-based matching and classification

- **NBLAST** (**Costa et al. 2016, Neuron 91(2):293–311, "NBLAST: Rapid, Sensitive Comparison of Neuronal Structure and Construction of Neuron Family Databases"**) compares neuron skeletons via point-and-tangent vectors; use `nat.nblast` (R) or `navis.nblast` (Python). For **LM↔EM** you use "synapse-blind" NBLAST on skeletons only. NBLAST against a hemilineage-labeled reference (MANC skeletons in JRC2018_VNC) yields a ranked list whose top hits' hemilineage labels vote for the query.
- **NeuronBridge** (Clements et al. 2024) provides web + programmatic LM↔EM search via color-depth MIP search and PPPM — a fast way to get candidate EM matches.
- **Complementary descriptors:** morphology feature vectors (Sholl, branch statistics), **TMD / persistence diagrams** (Kanari et al.), graph neural networks on skeletons, and contrastive embeddings can supplement NBLAST, especially for coarse hemilineage grouping.
- **Mapping match → hemilineage:** given a matched MANC body ID, query neuPrint (`manc`) with `neuprint-python` and read the `hemilineage`, `somaNeuromere`, and `entryNerve` fields. This is the concrete label-transfer step.
- **Existing hemilineage classifiers from morphology:** none is published as a turnkey tool for LM images; hemilineage assignment in connectomes has been done semi-manually using tract + soma cluster + morphology. This is your development gap.

### 6. Tract / bundle-based features

Neurons of a hemilineage enter the neuropil through a **shared primary neurite bundle** and have **clustered cell bodies** — a property exploited by connectome annotators and a very strong classification feature. **Court et al. 2020 (Neuron)** provides the standard VNC neuropil segmentation and tract nomenclature for JRC2018_VNC, giving named compartments and tracts you can use as landmarks. Encode, per neuron: soma neuromere and 3D soma position, the identity/coordinates of the primary neurite tract it joins, and its neuropil innervation profile (fraction of arbor per named compartment).

### 7. Practical pipeline architecture

Recommended end-to-end flow:
1. **Image QC** — check channels, resolution, nc82 quality; discard poorly stained stacks.
2. **Registration** — align nc82 to JRC2018_VNC_Unisex (CMTK/ANTs); apply transform to signal channels.
3. **Instance segmentation** — PatchPerPix / PPPM (or 3D U-Net + instance post-processing) to extract single neurons.
4. **Skeletonization** — SWC per neuron (Vaa3D/APP2, or skeletonize masks in navis).
5. **Feature extraction** — soma neuromere + position; primary neurite tract entry (vs. Court et al. compartments); neuropil innervation vector; NBLAST scores vs. hemilineage reference library.
6. **Classifier** — start with **k-NN / NBLAST top-k voting**, then random forest / gradient boosting on the combined feature vector, optionally a neural net; weight tract-entry features highly.
7. **Confidence + human-in-the-loop** — calibrated probability, abstain below threshold, route uncertain cases to expert review in neuroglancer / VVDviewer.

**Reference/training set:** transform MANC (and FANC) skeletons into JRC2018_VNC to create a labeled library; augment with hemilineage-specific driver-line MCFO images; hold out neurons/segments for evaluation.

**Software stacks:** Python (navis, flybrains, neuprint-python, fafbseg, cloud-volume, pyroglancer, PatchPerPixMatch); R (nat, nat.nblast, natverse, malevnc/manc); Fiji/ImageJ (SNT, color-depth plugins); viewers (VVDviewer, neuroglancer).

**Evaluation & pitfalls:** report per-hemilineage precision/recall, confusion matrix, top-k accuracy; expect confusion among morphologically similar hemilineages, developmental/individual variability, left-right asymmetry, partial MCFO labeling, and segmentation errors as dominant error sources. Cross-check ambiguous calls against neurotransmitter/TF driver expectations.

### 8. Related prior efforts
- Connectome annotation pipelines (Jefferis/natverse; Janelia FlyEM MANC team) — the closest existing "assign hemilineage" workflows, but connectome-based and semi-manual.
- Brain lineage atlases (Ito et al. systematic nomenclature; Yu et al.; Lovick/Wong et al.) — methodological transfer for lineage/clone-based morphology grouping.
- LM↔EM matching code: NeuronBridge, PatchPerPixMatch (Kainmueller lab GitHub), color-depth search (FlyLight/Janelia GitHub).

## Recommendations
1. **Phase 0 (2–4 weeks): stand up ground truth.** Pull MANC via neuprint-python, extract skeletons + `hemilineage`/`somaNeuromere`/`entryNerve`, and transform them into JRC2018_VNC_Unisex with navis/flybrains. Deliverable: a hemilineage-labeled reference skeleton library in LM space.
2. **Phase 1: registration + segmentation baseline.** Register a pilot set of MCFO VNC stacks (nc82) to JRC2018_VNC; run PatchPerPixMatch/NeuronBridge for candidate EM matches. Benchmark: fraction of neurons with a confident top match.
3. **Phase 2: feature-based classifier.** Add soma-neuromere, primary-neurite-tract-entry, and neuropil-innervation features; train k-NN/random forest; weight tract entry heavily. Benchmark: ≥80% top-1 hemilineage accuracy on held-out MANC-derived neurons before touching real LM.
4. **Phase 3: LM validation + human-in-the-loop.** Validate on hemilineage-specific driver-line MCFO images (known labels); deploy with calibrated abstention and neuroglancer review. Benchmark: precision on high-confidence calls ≥95%.
5. **Change triggers:** if NBLAST-only accuracy plateaus, add TMD/GNN embeddings; if similar-hemilineage confusion dominates, up-weight tract/soma features or add neurotransmitter priors from driver identity.

## Caveats
- Core citations were verified during enrichment (Harris et al. 2015 eLife 4:e04493; Takemura et al. 2024 eLife 13:RP97769; Marin et al. 2024 eLife 13:RP97766; Costa et al. 2016 Neuron 91(2):293–311; Nern et al. 2015 PNAS 112(22):E2967–E2976; Bogovic et al. 2020 PLOS ONE 15(12):e0236495). A few remaining identifiers (Court et al. 2020 volume/DOI, Lacin et al. 2019 DOI, Clements et al. 2024 and PatchPerPix DOIs, exact S3 bucket names) should be confirmed against the primary sources before formal publication.
- No turnkey LM-image hemilineage classifier is known to exist; the classifier and confidence calibration are genuine development work.
- MANC is male; FANC is female — sex differences and template choice can bias matches; validate across both.
- MCFO labeling is stochastic and often partial, which limits recall and can bias segmentation.
